# Intent to Calculation Mapping

Canonical reference for how every `FinancialIntent` value flows into financial
calculations, forecasting, and dashboard display.

Read alongside [FINANCIAL_MODEL.md](./FINANCIAL_MODEL.md) which defines the formulas.

---

## Master mapping table

| Intent | Affects Business Profit? | Affects True Cashflow? | Reporting Bucket | Forecast Treatment | Dashboard Display |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `freelance_income` | **Revenue ↑** | In ↑ | Business Revenue | Weighted avg + seasonal | Business Revenue card |
| `salary` | **Revenue ↑** | In ↑ | Business Revenue | Weighted avg (stable) | Business Revenue card |
| `passive_income` | No | In ↑ | Passive Income | Rolling 3-month avg | Secondary income line |
| `refund` | Costs ↓ (if business) | In ↑ | Refund / Cost reduction | Do not forecast | One-off annotation |
| `business_expense` | **Costs ↑** | Out ↑ | Business Costs | Weighted avg | Business Costs card |
| `tax_payment` | **Costs ↑** | Out ↑ | Tax & Compliance | Fixed-cycle spike model | Tax card (separate) |
| `subscription` | **Costs ↑** | Out ↑ | Business Costs (recurring) | Fixed commitment | Part of Business Costs |
| `personal_expense` | No | Out ↑ | Personal Spending | Rolling 3-month avg | Personal Burn Rate card |
| `family_support` | No | **Out ↑ ← WAS INVISIBLE** | Personal Spending | Rolling 3-month avg | Personal Burn Rate card |
| `loan_repayment` | No | **Out ↑ ← WAS INVISIBLE** | Debt Service | Fixed commitment | Debt Service card |
| `savings_transfer` | No | No (within ecosystem) | Savings Movement | Optional: track goal | Savings card |
| `investment` | No | No (within ecosystem) | Investment Activity | Optional: track goal | Investment card |
| `owner_draw` | No | No (within ecosystem) | Owner Draw | Do not forecast | Owner Draw annotation |
| `internal_transfer` | No | No | Excluded entirely | Exclude | Not shown |

## Per-intent deep dives

### `freelance_income`

**Financial meaning:** Money earned by delivering work to clients. This is the core revenue
of a freelance business.

**Business profit:** Revenue. Every euro here increases profit.

**Cashflow:** Positive. Increases available liquid cash.

**Forecast:** Use the existing weighted-average algorithm (recent 3 months weight 3×,
months 4–9 weight 2×, older weight 1×). Apply seasonal adjustment when ≥12 months of
data exist (30% blend at 12–23 months, 50% blend at 24+ months). This is the most
volatile input and the current algorithm handles it correctly.

**Dashboard:** Primary "Business Revenue" card. Also contributes to "Profit Margin" %.

**Known edge case:** A Stripe payout that bundles multiple client invoices appears as one
transaction. The intent is correctly `freelance_income` regardless of how many clients
contributed to the payout.

---

### `salary`

**Financial meaning:** Regular income from an employer or long-term contract with a
predictable pay cycle.

**Business profit:** Revenue. Treated identically to `freelance_income` in the P&L.

**Cashflow:** Positive. More predictable than freelance income.

**Forecast:** Weighted average but with higher recency bias — if salary has been stable
for the last 3 months, use that value rather than a longer-window average. This prevents
historical salary changes from distorting current projections.

**Dashboard:** Same "Business Revenue" card as `freelance_income`. Intelligence engine
can break these out in the income-type detection: a user with salary-dominant income
should receive different insight language than a project-based freelancer.

---

### `passive_income`

**Financial meaning:** Dividends, savings interest, rental income. Money generated by
assets, not by work.

**Business profit:** No effect. Passive income is not business operating revenue. Including
it in business profit would inflate the metric and mislead the user about how their
freelance business is actually performing.

**Cashflow:** Positive. Real money arriving in the account.

**Forecast:** Rolling 3-month average. Dividend income is lumpy (quarterly payments);
interest income is stable. A simple recent-average captures both cases better than the
long-window weighted average used for volatile freelance income.

**Dashboard:** Secondary "Other Income" or "Passive Income" line below Business Revenue.
Do not include in the headline Business Revenue card — it would conflate investment returns
with business earnings.

---

### `refund`

**Financial meaning:** Money returned by a vendor or platform. Offsets a previous expense.

**Business profit:** Partial. If the original expense was a business expense, the refund
reduces business costs. If the original expense was personal, the refund reduces personal
spending. The system cannot currently determine which original expense a refund offsets
without linking transactions, so refunds are treated as income and reduce true cashflow
outflow.

**Cashflow:** Positive.

**Forecast:** Do not forecast. Refunds are non-recurring and unpredictable by definition.
Including them in forecast baselines would cause the model to assume refunds will always
arrive, understating projected expenses.

**Dashboard:** Show as a line item in the income section annotated as "Refund". Do not
include in Business Revenue headline.

---

### `business_expense`

**Financial meaning:** A cost directly incurred to deliver work or run the freelance
business. Equipment, co-working space, professional services, tools.

**Business profit:** Cost. Every euro here reduces profit.

**Cashflow:** Negative. Real cash outflow.

**Forecast:** Weighted average. Business costs tend to be more stable than income — a
freelancer's tooling and service costs do not swing as much as client revenue.

**Dashboard:** "Business Costs" card. Also contributes to "Profit Margin" calculation.

---

### `tax_payment`

**Financial meaning:** Payments to tax authorities. Income tax, VAT, social charges
(URSSAF), corporation tax, estimated tax payments.

**Business profit:** Cost. Tax is a cost of operating as a freelancer. It reduces profit.

**Cashflow:** Negative. Real cash outflow, often large and lumpy.

**Forecast:** Fixed-cycle spike model. Tax is not paid smoothly every month — it arrives
in predictable windows (quarterly estimated payments, annual self-assessment, etc.). The
forecast should:
1. Calculate average annual tax spend from history.
2. Identify which months historically had tax payments.
3. Project those same months as elevated in the forecast.

Do not use a simple monthly average for tax — this would smooth a €3,200 January payment
into €267/month and badly understate the January cashflow impact.

**Dashboard:** Separate "Tax & Compliance" card. Tax is a line item freelancers must
plan for separately. Mixing it with other business costs obscures the tax obligation.

---

### `subscription`

**Financial meaning:** Recurring software or service payments. GitHub, Figma, Adobe,
cloud hosting, SaaS tools used in the business.

**Business profit:** Cost. Subscriptions are operating costs of the business.

**Cashflow:** Negative.

**Forecast:** Fixed commitment model. Once a subscription is established, it repeats at
a fixed amount. Use the actual last-known value, not an average. Flag new subscriptions
(first occurrence in the last 3 months with no prior history) as "new recurring cost".

**Dashboard:** Part of "Business Costs" card. The intelligence engine can surface a
"Subscription Spend" sub-total since subscriptions are a category freelancers frequently
overspend on without noticing.

---

### `personal_expense`

**Financial meaning:** Personal spending — food, health, transport, housing, utilities,
entertainment. Money spent on personal life, not the business.

**Business profit:** No effect. Personal costs are not business costs. Including them
in the P&L would show a business losing money whenever the owner buys groceries.

**Cashflow:** Negative. Real cash outflow even though it doesn't affect business profit.

**Forecast:** Rolling 3-month average. Personal spending patterns are the most stable
of all intent categories — lifestyle costs change slowly and predictably. A short window
(3 months) captures current lifestyle better than a long historical average that might
include a different life stage.

**Dashboard:** "Personal Burn Rate" card. This is the amount the freelancer spends on
themselves each month independent of their business. Key for understanding how much
business profit they need to sustain their lifestyle.

---

### `family_support`

**Financial meaning:** Money sent to family — remittances via WorldRemit, TaptapSend,
Western Union, or direct bank transfers. This is currently **completely invisible** in
the financial model because it is classified as `transactionType: "transfer"` and excluded
from all calculations.

**Business profit:** No effect. Family support is a personal financial commitment, not
a business cost.

**Cashflow:** **Negative. This is the most important correction in the new model.**
If a user sends €600/month to family, their true available cash is €600/month less than
the dashboard currently shows. The current `netCashflow` is systematically overstated for
any user with regular remittances.

**Forecast:** Rolling 3-month average. Remittances are typically regular, fixed-amount
commitments — among the most predictable personal cash outflows.

**Dashboard:** Part of "Personal Burn Rate" card. The intelligence engine should
specifically call this out: "Your family support commitments total €X/month" is a distinct
and meaningful insight that no generic "expenses" metric captures.

---

### `loan_repayment`

**Financial meaning:** Debt service — mortgage, personal loan, student debt, car finance,
credit card debt repayment. Currently **invisible** in the financial model for the same
reason as `family_support`.

**Business profit:** No effect. Debt repayment is a personal financial commitment.
(The interest component of a loan repayment is technically a cost, but the system cannot
separate principal from interest without structured loan data — treat the full payment
as a personal obligation.)

**Cashflow:** **Negative. Second-most important correction in the new model.**
A €1,500/month mortgage payment is a real cash outflow. Currently excluded from all
calculations.

**Forecast:** Fixed commitment model (highest confidence of all categories). Loan
payments do not change month to month. Use the actual last-known payment value. Flag
if the payment amount changes significantly (potential rate change or refinancing).

**Dashboard:** Separate "Debt Service" card. This is a non-negotiable fixed obligation
that the user cannot reduce month to month. It should be shown separately from both
business costs (it is not a business cost) and personal spending (it is not discretionary).

---

### `savings_transfer`

**Financial meaning:** Deliberate movement of money from a current/checking account to a
savings account — Hello Bank, ISA contributions, pension contributions, emergency fund
top-ups.

**Business profit:** No effect.

**Cashflow:** No effect on total wealth. Money moves within the user's financial ecosystem
from one pocket to another. Reduces liquid cash but increases savings balance — net worth
is unchanged.

**Forecast:** Optional goal-based model. If the user has a consistent savings habit
(e.g. ISA contribution every April), the forecast can flag that month. Do not include in
the core cashflow forecast.

**Dashboard:** "Savings" card (existing). The existing `totalSavings` metric already
captures this. With intent, savings_transfer can be distinguished from investment within
the savings bucket.

---

### `investment`

**Financial meaning:** Capital deployed to investment platforms — stocks, ETFs, crypto,
index funds via Trading212, DeGiro, Vanguard, Coinbase, etc.

**Business profit:** No effect. Investment is not a business cost.

**Cashflow:** No effect on total wealth (money moves from bank to investment portfolio,
which is still the user's asset). Reduces liquid cash.

**Forecast:** Optional. If the user makes regular investment contributions (e.g. monthly
DCA into an ETF), the forecast can model this as a recurring allocation. Do not include
in core cashflow forecast.

**Dashboard:** "Investment Activity" card. Distinct from savings — investment carries
market risk that savings transfers do not. The user should see these separately.

---

### `owner_draw`

**Financial meaning:** The business owner withdrawing money from their business account
for personal use. In a single-account freelancer context, this is often a deliberate
transfer from a "business" portion of the account to a personal account.

**Business profit:** No effect. Owner draw is a distribution of profit that has already
been earned and taxed. It is not a business expense — treating it as one would
understate profit.

**Cashflow:** No effect on total wealth (within-ecosystem movement). Reduces business
liquidity.

**Forecast:** Do not forecast. Owner draws are discretionary decisions, not recurring
commitments. Projecting them forward would assume the user will always draw the same
amount, which is not a reliable assumption.

**Dashboard:** "Owner Draw" annotation. Show as a separate line item in a financial
allocation section (alongside savings and investment). The user should see how much
profit they extracted from the business each month.

---

### `internal_transfer`

**Financial meaning:** Movement between two accounts owned by the same person —
Monzo main → Revolut travel card, pot-to-pot transfers, round-ups. No money leaves
or enters the user's financial ecosystem.

**Business profit:** No effect.

**Cashflow:** No effect.

**Forecast:** Exclude entirely.

**Dashboard:** Do not display. Internal transfers have no financial meaning and
showing them would create noise. They should remain invisible in all views.

## Formulas reference

```
Business Revenue      = Σ amount WHERE intent IN ('freelance_income', 'salary')
Business Costs        = Σ amount WHERE intent IN ('business_expense', 'tax_payment', 'subscription')
Business Profit       = Business Revenue − Business Costs
Profit Margin %       = Business Profit / Business Revenue × 100

Personal Spend        = Σ amount WHERE intent IN ('personal_expense', 'family_support')
Debt Service          = Σ amount WHERE intent = 'loan_repayment'
Total Obligations     = Personal Spend + Debt Service

Savings Movement      = Σ amount WHERE intent = 'savings_transfer'
Investment Flow       = Σ amount WHERE intent = 'investment'
Owner Draw            = Σ amount WHERE intent = 'owner_draw'

Passive Income        = Σ amount WHERE intent = 'passive_income'
Refunds               = Σ amount WHERE intent = 'refund'

True Net Cashflow     = Business Revenue + Passive Income + Refunds
                      − Business Costs − Personal Spend − Debt Service

(all amounts are absolute values; sign direction is implicit from the bucket)
```

## Graceful fallback when intent is null

Until `recategorize-all` is run on existing transactions, `intent` is null for all
historical rows. Every function that computes intent-aware metrics must:

1. Return `null` (not `0`) when no intent data exists for the queried period.
2. The UI must display a "data not yet classified" state rather than showing €0.
3. The existing `totalIncome`, `totalExpenses`, `netCashflow` remain the primary metrics
   until intent coverage reaches a meaningful threshold (suggested: >80% of transactions).

The `getCategorizationHealth()` function already returns `totalCount` which can be used
to compute intent coverage:

```ts
const intentCoverage = (totalCount - nullIntentCount) / totalCount * 100;
// Show intent KPIs only when intentCoverage > 80
```

## Edge cases

**Mixed-intent transaction:** A single Stripe payout that includes a client refund and
a service fee deduction. The system cannot split one transaction into multiple intents —
the intent of the whole transaction is `freelance_income`. Accept this as a known
limitation; the amounts are small relative to the total.

**Refund origin unknown:** A refund transaction cannot be automatically linked to its
original expense. The refund intent is applied, but whether it was a business or personal
refund requires user context. Default: treat as income reduction, do not assign to
business costs or personal spend.

**Partial loan payments:** Some months a user may overpay or underpay. Fixed commitment
forecasting should use a trailing 3-month average (not the single last value) to smooth
legitimate variation while still treating loan repayment as a near-fixed cost.

**Owner draw in single-account setup:** If the user has only one account, every
withdrawal could be considered an "owner draw" from the business. In practice,
`owner_draw` is only classified when the description matches specific patterns or the
user explicitly sets it. Do not apply `owner_draw` broadly to all outflows.
